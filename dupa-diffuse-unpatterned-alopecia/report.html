Meta-review synthesis

Across three rounds the loop converged on the defining clinical reality of diffuse unpatterned alopecia: it is a diagnosis of exclusion, and the highest-yield interventions are the ones that respect that structure rather than the highest-Elo systemic bets. The final Elo table is stratified almost exactly along this line. The top tier (1547-1548) is occupied entirely by one lineage - the biomarker-gated micronutrient correction protocol (H008 -> H013 -> H028 -> H040) - whose final lean form H040 drops the structurally underpowered L-lysine arm and demotes the contested 40-70 ng/mL ferritin range from a claimed therapeutic threshold to an operational trigger under interventional test. External validation is what makes H040 the lead pick: zero prior alopecia trials, PubMed novelty score 1.0, confidence 3, verified associational citations (PMIDs 20021982, 32792884, 16635664, 24887725), near-zero cost, and a safety case that IS the gating (no iron in non-deficient patients without a source-of-loss workup). The single strongest near-term experiment in the whole run is simply executing H040: a 16-week repletion trial in confirmed-deficient diffuse-loss patients with shed count and standardized densitometry endpoints versus a non-deficient reference arm. It tests whether the entire deficiency-mimic thesis is causal, cheaply, and the answer re-routes a large patient fraction either way. For the true androgen-driven remnant, H041 is the right final form of the androgen lineage: it merges H001 and H002 into a sequential program (topical/low-dose dutasteride first with a densitometry futility threshold, oral escalation, then topical bicalutamide only behind a preclinical skin-PK gate). Validation cuts both ways here and the synthesis should be candid about it: dutasteride has the strongest disease-target overlap in the run (SRD5A2 0.70, SRD5A1 0.51, SRD5A3 0.50 via OpenTargets) but also 10 prior alopecia trials up to phase 4 and 280 PubMed co-mentions - it is validated pharmacology, not a novel bet, and the dossier itself warns 5AR-inhibitor response in DUPA is anecdotal and probably attenuated. H035 (TSH-gated levothyroxine) is the cheapest completeness pick: it closes the last uncovered exclusion niche (the thyroid mimic, PMID 38181279) with confidence 3 and benign lab-guided dosing. H043 is included as portfolio plumbing rather than a repurposing bet - its own review concedes zero novelty (349 co-mentions, approved AA pharmacology) - but the double-gated incognita rule-out arm is the only entry preventing autoimmune mimics from being misrouted into androgen-axis trials, and in a diagnosis-of-exclusion disease that routing has real expected value. Where it is guessing: everything mechanistic beyond those anchors. The oxidative-stress axis (H044) is two unproven inferences deep and survives only as a Stage-A pharmacodynamic gate; nicorandil, fluridil, estradiol, and metformin never rose above Elo 1508 for good reason. 1. iron (ferrous sulfate) (H008) Novel candidate androgen

ChEMBL: n/a · Elo 1548 · confidence 3/5 · route: oral · origin: generation (round 1) · theme: Diagnosis-of-exclusion mimic capture via biomarker-gated…

Mechanism
Target sub-population: the deficiency-mimic / chronic telogen effluvium sub-population of the diffuse-thinning presentation - specifically patients with confirmed low ferritin - rather than true androgen-driven DUPA. The hair matrix is among the most proliferative tissues in the body and is exquisitely sensitive to iron availability; low iron stores (even without anemia) impair ribonucleotide reductase and matrix keratinocyte proliferation, shortening anagen and increasing telogen shedding. Oral ferrous sulfate repletion, dosed to a target ferritin and paired with a source-of-loss workup, removes a treatable driver of diffuse shedding that otherwise masquerades as DUPA.
Evidence
PMID 20021982: women with diffuse telogen hair loss had significantly lower ferritin (16.3 vs 60.3 ng/mL) and transferrin saturation than matched controls. PMID 32792884: mean ferritin significantly lower in diffuse-hair-loss patients vs healthy controls (14.72 vs 25.30 ng/mL); authors recommend ferritin evaluation before treatment. PMID 16635664: review of iron deficiency and its potential relationship to hair loss, with ferritin as the confirmatory test. This is a biomarker-stratified correction hypothesis, per the dossier's guidance. (refs: PMID 16635664, PMID 20021982, PMID 32792884)
Safety
The dossier warns against blind supplementation: excess iron is harmful and inappropriate in non-deficient men and postmenopausal women, where iron deficiency also mandates a GI blood-loss workup (PMID 16635664). Hypothesis is explicitly gated on confirmed low ferritin with a defined repletion target and monitoring; GI upset and constipation are the common tolerability issues.
Novelty rationale
Not novel as a concept - iron-hair associations are long-studied - but the hypothesis is the evidence-graded version the dossier asks for: a ferritin-stratified correction protocol within the diagnostic-exclusion workup, not blind supplementation. Its role in the portfolio is to capture the large, treatable mimic population.
