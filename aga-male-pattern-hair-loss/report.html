Misoprostol (H005) is included from mid-table (Elo 1484) as the high-risk/high-novelty slot: zero trials, novelty 0.14, and it attacks a human-validated prostaglandin pathway from a direction no prior program has tried. 1. BICALUTAMIDE (H001) Novel candidate androgen

ChEMBL: CHEMBL409 · Elo 1548 · confidence 3/5 · route: topical · origin: generation (round 1) · theme: Direct androgen-axis blockade

Mechanism
Bicalutamide is a pure non-steroidal androgen receptor (AR) antagonist that competitively occupies the AR ligand-binding pocket and blocks AR dimerization and nuclear translocation. Per the dossier (section 1), DHT-driven AR signaling in dermal papilla cells is the initiating event of follicle miniaturization in AGA, and the excerpt literature confirms that preventing AR activation/nuclear translocation rescues dermal papilla cells from DHT-induced apoptosis, senescence, and hair-cycle arrest. Topically delivered bicalutamide could block AR locally in dermal papilla cells at the receptor level, downstream of 5-alpha-reductase, catching DHT from both type I and type II isoenzymes.
Evidence
PMID 39306884 demonstrates that competitive AR binding preventing AR dimerization/nuclear translocation alleviates DHT-induced dermal papilla cell damage and reverses androgenetic alopecia in a DHT-induced mouse model, directly validating receptor-level blockade as a therapeutic strategy. PMID 24244503 shows AR signaling drives premature senescence of dermal papilla cells specifically in balding-prone scalp regions, and AR knockdown abolishes the effect — supporting local AR antagonism as disease-modifying. (refs: PMID 39306884, PMID 24244503)
Safety
Oral bicalutamide causes gynecomastia, breast tenderness, and hepatotoxicity — unacceptable systemically for a cosmetic indication. Topical formulation at low concentration with minimal systemic absorption is essential; percutaneous absorption must be characterized. Best suited initially for male pattern AGA where oral anti-androgens are intolerable; avoid in pregnancy-exposed partners. Local skin irritation is the main expected topical risk.
Novelty rationale
Systemic AR antagonists are unusable in men due to feminizing side effects, which is why receptor-level blockade is absent from male AGA therapy despite being the most direct mechanism. A topically restricted bicalutamide formulation for male AGA is a genuinely new angle — fluridil attempted this niche but is a weaker, less-characterized antagonist and is not widely approved.

4. DEFEROXAMINE (H021) Novel candidate —

ChEMBL: CHEMBL556 · Elo 1532 · confidence 2/5 · route: topical · origin: generation (round 2) · theme: Iron chelation and oxidative-stress control…

Mechanism
Deferoxamine is an iron chelator that, applied topically, stabilizes HIF-1alpha by chelating the iron cofactor of prolyl hydroxylases, driving a local pro-angiogenic VEGF program. Miniaturized AGA follicles lose their supporting microvasculature as they shrink; restoring perifollicular angiogenic signaling could support re-enlargement. Additionally, iron chelation removes the Fenton-reaction substrate, reducing hydroxyl-radical generation - directly relevant to the oxidative stress and androgen-driven DNA damage reported in balding dermal papilla cells.
Evidence
PMID 24244503 demonstrates DNA damage (gamma-H2AX foci) and premature senescence in androgen-exposed balding dermal papilla cells, a phenotype oxidative stress contributes to. PMID 40110814 lists oxidative-stress-state changes among AGA pathogenic factors. The dossier (section 8) describes ROS and senescence in balding papilla. No excerpt tests deferoxamine on follicles; the angiogenesis rationale comes from its known HIF pharmacology in wound-healing contexts. (refs: PMID 24244503, PMID 40110814)
Safety
Systemic deferoxamine (infusion) carries hypotension, and auditory/ocular toxicity with chronic use - irrelevant at topical cosmetic exposure. Topical deferoxamine has been used experimentally on wounds with acceptable tolerability; it is hydrophilic, so penetration enhancement would be needed. Local irritation is the main expected risk.
Novelty rationale
Pharmacologic HIF-1alpha stabilization to rebuild the perifollicular vascular niche is a mechanism absent from the entire round-1 set and from AGA practice; the same molecule secondarily addresses oxidative DNA damage, a documented balding-papilla phenotype, making it a two-pronged but single-drug proposal.
Reviews
  • plausibility 6/10, evidence 5/10, safety 7/10, novelty 8/10 — verdict revise. The strongest of this set, but with two adversarial points. First, delivery: deferoxamine is a highly hydrophilic ~560 Da iron chelator with notoriously poor passive skin penetration; the proposal waves at 'penetration enhancement' without acknowledging that achieving follicular concentrations sufficient to chelate prolyl-hydroxylase iron in dermal papilla is the make-or-break experiment, and no data are offered. The cited evidence checks out (PMID 24244503 verified - androgen/AR-driven premature senescence with gamma-H2AX in dermal papilla; PMID 40110814 verified - oxidative stress state listed among AGA factors), and the wound-healing HIF pharmacology is real but transplant-naive. The dossier explicitly flags that other approved KATP openers are worth proposing only if topically viable; nicorandil is an approved antianginal with a dual KATP-opener/nitrate profile that has never been systematically developed as a topical hair agent. (refs: PMID 40110814)
Safety
Oral nicorandil causes headache, hypotension, and rare gastrointestinal/mucosal ulceration — systemic exposure must be minimized. Topical scalp application at low concentration is the only acceptable route; local flushing or headache from absorbed drug should be monitored in early studies. No teratogenicity class concern comparable to valproate.
Novelty rationale
This is a mechanistic successor to minoxidil rather than a copy: it delivers the KATP-opening pharmacophore directly, bypassing the SULT1A1 activation step that limits and varies minoxidil response. No approved topical KATP opener other than minoxidil exists for hair.
Reviews
  • plausibility 6/10, evidence 3/10, safety 6/10, novelty 5/10 — verdict revise. An elegant-sounding 'SULT1A1 bypass' rationale with zero direct evidence behind it. Nothing cited shows nicorandil acts on dermal papilla or follicular KATP channels: the hypothesis assumes the follicular channel subtype matches the vascular SUR2-containing channels nicorandil preferentially opens, which is unproven — if the follicle's relevant channel is a different SUR/Kir assembly, the whole premise collapses. Minoxidil's mechanism is itself 'not fully resolved' per the dossier (vasodilation vs papilla signaling vs beta-catenin effects), so claiming a mechanistic successor to an unresolved mechanism is overreach; nicorandil may replicate minoxidil's failures rather than its successes, and its nitrate moiety adds tachyphylaxis/tolerance and headache burden minoxidil lacks. Novelty is also softer than claimed — this is fundamentally a second topical KATP opener, i.e., adjacent to a 40-year-old drug class, and 'more uniform response' is a marketing hypothesis until follicular potency is demonstrated. Needs follicular KATP subtype/pharmacology data and a head-to-head ex vivo follicle comparison against minoxidil sulfate before it merits resources.

32. melatonin (H007) Known hair-related use —

ChEMBL: CHEMBL45 · Elo 1468 · confidence 2/5 · route: topical · origin: generation (round 1)

Mechanism
Melatonin is a potent antioxidant and free-radical scavenger with receptors expressed in the hair follicle. Per the dossier (section 8), balding dermal papilla cells show oxidative stress and androgen-driven ROS production, and oxidative stress from UV/pollution compounds follicle damage. Topical melatonin could reduce oxidative damage in dermal papilla cells and follicular epithelium, and melatonin-receptor signaling has been implicated in anagen regulation in follicle biology.
Evidence
PMID 40110814 explicitly lists altered oxidative-stress state among the mechanisms involved in AGA pathogenesis. PMID 24244503 shows androgen/AR-driven DNA damage and premature senescence in dermal papilla cells — a phenotype tightly coupled to ROS accumulation, which a locally applied antioxidant could mitigate. (refs: PMID 40110814, PMID 24244503)
Safety
Topical melatonin has an excellent safety record and has already been used in cosmetic scalp lotions; systemic absorption from scalp application is minimal and melatonin is non-toxic even at supraphysiologic exposure. Main risk is trivial (local irritation, theoretical daytime sedation with excessive application).
Novelty rationale
Flagged: topical melatonin has prior small-scale alopecia studies and existing cosmetic use, so it is not strictly novel. It is included as a safe, oxidative-stress-targeted candidate that addresses a pathogenic arm (ROS-driven papilla damage) untouched by finasteride or minoxidil and is suitable for combination regimens.
Reviews
  • plausibility 4/10, evidence 2/10, safety 9/10, novelty 3/10 — verdict reject. This is generic antioxidant hand-waving dressed up as a mechanism. Neither cited paper is about melatonin: PMID 40110814 merely lists oxidative stress among AGA mechanisms and PMID 24244503 documents androgen-driven senescence in dermal papilla cells — neither establishes that scavenging ROS reverses miniaturization, and the antioxidant rationale has failed repeatedly across dermatology because oxidative stress in AGA is more likely a downstream marker than a driver. Topical melatonin already has prior small alopecia studies with marginal, methodologically weak results, and it sits in cosmetic scalp lotions today, so the novelty claim is thin. Safe and cheap, yes — but the pipeline gains nothing from a hypothesis whose entire evidentiary support is by-association. Fatally under-evidenced as written; would need direct preclinical proof of anagen rescue, not borrowed oxidative-stress citations.
